Analog Devices $ADI announced an agreement to acquire Alif Semiconductor for $1.35 billion in cash on Wednesday, adding the California-based edge AI chipmaker's microcontrollers and fusion processors to its portfolio.
The deal calls for Analog Devices to hand Alif stockholders $1.35 billion at closing, with a further earn-out of as much as $200 million available depending on contingencies, the company said. Directors at each company have signed off on the deal, which must still clear Hart-Scott-Rodino antitrust review before it can wrap up, a process Analog Devices anticipates completing by the end of 2026.
Alif, headquartered in Pleasanton, California, makes microcontrollers and fusion processors designed for real-time sensor fusion, low-latency inference, and on-device AI processing. Its silicon is already in production, with design wins across consumer and industrial customers, the company said.
Analog Devices said the acquisition advances what it calls Physical Intelligence — systems capable of sensing, reasoning, and acting locally in real time. The combination is intended to expand the company's reach across industrial, data center infrastructure, defense, energy, robotics, digital health, and wearable markets.
"By combining Alif's digital processing capabilities with our leadership in multi-modal sensing, signal processing, power, connectivity, and software, we can empower customers to create entirely new classes of secure, intelligent systems that sense, reason, and act locally in real time," Analog Devices CEO and Chair Vincent Roche said in a statement.
Reza Kazerounian, co-founder and president of Alif, said his company was built to rethink what a microcontroller could do in the AI era, integrating neural processing with connectivity, security, and power management. "Combined with ADI's deep physical-domain expertise and broad analog system capabilities, we can expand our reach to deliver the solutions that can power the future of Physical Intelligence," he said in a statement.
PJT Partners is serving as financial advisor to Analog Devices, with Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz as legal counsel. Qatalyst Partners is advising Alif, with DLA Piper as legal counsel, the company said.
The deal marks Analog Devices' second major acquisition targeting AI-related markets this year. The company earlier agreed to acquire Empower Semiconductor for $1.5 billion in cash, a deal aimed at power delivery constraints in AI data center design. That transaction is also expected to close in the second half of 2026. Analog Devices reported revenue of more than $11 billion in its most recent fiscal year, according to the company.

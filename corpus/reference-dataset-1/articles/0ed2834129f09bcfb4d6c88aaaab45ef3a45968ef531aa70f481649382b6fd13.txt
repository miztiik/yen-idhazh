OpenAI is expanding cooperation with Samsung Electronics across semiconductors and enterprise AI services, with joint research on next-generation chips at the center of the relationship, according to Reuters.
ChatGPT Enterprise usage at South Korean companies surged about 28-fold in a year as OpenAI deepens semiconductor and AI services ties with Samsung
Speaking at a Seoul press conference on Wednesday, Harrison Kim, General Manager of OpenAI Korea, told reporters that chip development was one area where the two companies had achieved meaningful progress together. "One of the areas where we have made the most progress and gained the most recognition with Samsung Electronics is our joint production and research on the next-generation chips we are developing," Kim said, declining to go into further detail. A Samsung Electronics spokesperson declined to comment on matters involving its customers.
Harrison Kim also described Samsung Electronics as "one of the largest-scale deployments of ChatGPT globally," noting that Samsung employees in South Korea and overseas use the platform across research and development, marketing, and sales.
The number of ChatGPT Enterprise users across South Korean businesses and institutions had grown roughly 28 times over by late August compared with the same point the prior year, according to OpenAI. The company declined to provide absolute user numbers. As of late last year, no country outside the United States had more paying ChatGPT subscribers than South Korea, OpenAI said.
Kim said the growing sophistication of AI hardware would keep pushing up demand for memory chips, and that working closely with South Korean semiconductor suppliers had become a core focus for OpenAI's office in the country. Last year, Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix signed letters of intent to supply memory chips for OpenAI's Stargate data center project.
OpenAI unveiled its first custom AI chip, called Jalapeno, in June. Built in collaboration with Broadcom $AVGO to handle AI inference — which involves processing inputs to generate responses in tools like ChatGPT — the chip will be produced at facilities belonging to Taiwan's TSMC $TSM.
Samsung has also been shipping samples of its HBM4E high-bandwidth memory chip to major global customers, becoming the first company to distribute the next-generation AI memory product. High-bandwidth memory chips are used in AI accelerators including Nvidia $NVDA's Rubin and Google $GOOGL's Ironwood Tensor Processing Unit, according to Reuters. Samsung's customers include AMD $AMD, Nvidia, and Google.
On the enterprise side, Samsung announced in June that employees within its Device eXperience division — the unit responsible for mobile phones, televisions, and home appliances — had been given approval to use ChatGPT, Google's Gemini, and Anthropic's Claude as part of their day-to-day work. OpenAI said it expects AI adoption to accelerate following the September 4 launch of GPT-6 Astra, its latest model.

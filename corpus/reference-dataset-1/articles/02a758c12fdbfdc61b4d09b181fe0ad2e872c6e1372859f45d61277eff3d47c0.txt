Provisional data released by the RBI on Wednesday showed FCNR(B) deposits brought in $127.23 billion, while overseas foreign currency borrowings (OFCBs) contributed $5.26 billion and external commercial borrowings (ECBs) $3.89 billion. The figures are subject to final reporting, accounting and reconciliation.

The latest data underscore the exceptionally strong response to the facility, which the RBI introduced on June 8 to attract foreign currency inflows and strengthen India’s external buffers. By August 21, banks had mobilised $65.4 billion through FCNR(B) deposits, while total inflows under the three routes had reached about $73 billion.
"...Given the magnitude of these inflows, its profitable deployment is likely to take some time and could intensify competition for quality borrowers," said Sachin Sachdeva, Vice President & Co-group Head of Financial Sector Ratings, at ICRA.
The inflows have added substantially to banking-system liquidity and strengthened the RBI’s foreign exchange firepower. India’s banking liquidity rose to ₹6.65 lakh crore on August 31, its highest level since May 2022, with the weighted average call rate falling to 4.98%, well below the repo rate.

The scale of mobilisation has far exceeded the response to the RBI’s 2013 FCNR(B) swap scheme, when banks raised about $26 billion through FCNR(B) deposits.
The RBI said the latest numbers are provisional, pending final reporting, accounting and reconciliation.

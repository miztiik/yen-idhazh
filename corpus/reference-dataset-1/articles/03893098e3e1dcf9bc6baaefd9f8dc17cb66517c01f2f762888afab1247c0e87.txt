One Nation One Election monstrous, unconstitutional: Chidambaram to parl panel
Former Union Minister P Chidambaram opposed the "One Nation, One Election" proposal before a parliamentary panel, calling it "monstrous", "unconstitutional" and "mindless". He argued that curtailing legislatures' five-year terms violates the Constitution's basic structure. TMC's Sagarika Ghosh also opposed the bills, while Padma awardees remained divided over simultaneous polls.
Former Union Minister P Chidambaram on Monday strongly opposed the move to hold simultaneous elections to the Lok Sabha and state assemblies before a joint committee of Parliament, terming the initiative "monstrous" and "unconstitutional."
The former home and finance minister also said that the proposal to hold nationwide polls under "One Nation, One Election" is a "mindless" exercise.
Sources said Chidambaram submitted his opinion while appearing before the joint committee of Parliament on the Constitution (129th Amendment) Bill, 2024, and the Union Territories Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2024, which aim to synchronise Lok Sabha and state assembly polls.
The Congress leader submitted that the bills on simultaneous polls are "unconstitutional." He argued that since any legislature is elected for a five-year term, curtailing its tenure directly violates the basic structure of the Constitution, sources aware of the deliberations said.
The senior Congress leader also said that his conscience does not allow him to support the bills and stressed that the government does not have the number (two-thirds majority) to pass the legislation, sources said.
TMC's Sagarika Ghosh, a member of the panel, also opposed the bill and said it would give "unbridled" power to the Election Commission.
A group of about 10 Padma awardees, including Tarlochan Singh, Navin Khanna, Meenakshi Jain and Niru Kumar, also appeared separately before the panel headed by BJP member PP Chaudhary.
While some of the Padma awardees supported the initiative of simultaneous polls, others opposed it, saying it may bring "chaos" in the country.
The bills were introduced in Lok Sabha in December 2024, following which they were referred to the joint committee for examination.
The bills were formulated after a high-level panel, headed by former president Ram Nath Kovind, recommended holding of Lok Sabha, state assembly and local body elections simultaneously in a phased manner. Union Home Minister Amit Shah was also a member of the high-level panel headed by Kovind.
Following the adoption of the Constitution, elections to the Lok Sabha and all State Legislative Assemblies were conducted simultaneously from 1951 to 1967.
The first general elections to the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies were held together in 1951-52, a practice that continued for three subsequent general elections in 1957, 1962, and 1967.
However, this cycle of synchronised elections was disrupted in 1968 and 1969 due to the premature dissolution of some state legislative assemblies.
The Fourth Lok Sabha was also dissolved prematurely in 1970, with fresh elections held in 1971. Unlike the First, Second, and Third Lok Sabha, which completed their full five-year terms, the Fifth Lok Sabha's term was extended until 1977 under Article 352 because of the declaration of Emergency.
Since then, only a few Lok Sabha terms have lasted the full five years, such as the Eighth, Tenth, Fourteenth, and Fifteenth. Others, including the Sixth, Seventh, Ninth, Eleventh, Twelfth, and Thirteenth, were dissolved early.
State Assemblies have faced similar disruptions over the years. Premature dissolutions and term extensions have become a recurring challenge.
These developments have firmly disrupted the cycle of simultaneous elections, leading to the current pattern of staggered electoral schedules across the country.

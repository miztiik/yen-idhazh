
The plan, when the question below asks for one.

  When you write both, write "summary" first and "visual" second. Finish the
  summary before you start the plan. You never type a number into the plan.

  Decide whether this item wants a picture at all. Nothing is the ordinary
  answer: two items in three carry no picture, and a picture that only decorates
  the prose costs a reader time and returns none.

  "decision" - "visual" or "none". If "none", leave "purpose", "type", "title"
  and "caption" null and every list empty, and say why in "why".

  "purpose" - what the reader is asked to do with it. Commit to this before you
  choose a type, or the type picks the purpose for you.

  "type" - the form.

  "encodings" - which elements fill which channel. Every channel is a key and
  you write all of them; a channel this picture does not draw gets an empty
  list.

  "element_ids" - every element the picture draws from, listed once.

  "labels" - the elements whose own words name the marks and the axes.

  "annotations" - the elements to mark first.

  "why" - why this form, or why nothing. Nobody sees this.

  "title" and "caption" - what the picture is called, and one sentence under
  it.

  "confidence" - how sure you are, between 0 and 1.

  Everywhere the plan points at something, copy an address from the item's
  table. Never type a figure, a position, a size or a colour. The picture is
  drawn from the item's own characters at the addresses you name, so an address
  the table does not carry draws nothing.

  Plan the item, not your summary. The summary is a compression and it has
  dropped things the table still holds.
